The flood problem

When both rivers run high

When Lake Ontario runs high, releasing more water can spare its shoreline. That water travels down the St. Lawrence to Lake St. Louis near Montréal, where the Ottawa River also arrives. Can the two operators make better use of the room downstream by planning together?

What is Plan 2014?

Plan 2014 is the International Joint Commission’s plan for regulating Lake Ontario outflow through the Moses–Saunders Dam. It sets weekly releases using lake levels, water supplies and conditions along the river. How the regulation board sets outflows

Our baseline, filtered Plan 2014, starts from the simulator’s rule-curve proposal. It passes through the native release limits and the same Ottawa feasibility check as the two model predictive controllers (MPCs).

Where the rivers meet

In the study, each Ottawa release takes one quarter-month to reach Lake St. Louis. Water already on its way at the start of the year counts toward the first arrival.

How we replay a flood spring

Simulator
The Lake Ontario–St. Lawrence River simulator supplies the lake balance, release limits and gauge levels. We add a synthetic Ottawa reservoir and two controllers. Original simulator · Coupled study
Time step
Four decisions per month, 48 per year. The operators execute the first planned releases, observe the next state and replan. Spring covers April through June, steps 13–24.
Competing objectives
High water threatens homes on both shorelines. Low water affects recreational boating. Water routed through turbines generates electricity. We report flood exposure, affected buildings, boating costs and hydropower value.
Hard limits in these runs
Releases must satisfy the native Lake Ontario rules, Ottawa storage and outlet bounds, and a combined downstream flow ceiling. Lake Ontario levels above 75.50 m incur a cost. Enforcing forecast stage limits at both shorelines is a separate experiment.

Lake Ontario

Hold water or release it?

At Moses–Saunders Dam, how much can a release change the lake level in a single quarter-month? With the same incoming supply, another 297 m³/s of outflow lowers the modeled end level by one centimetre.

Ottawa River

Two flows meet at Lake St. Louis

The Ottawa operator can store incoming water or release it. Its release reaches Lake St. Louis one decision later. How much room will be left there when that water arrives?

The experiments pair historical Lake Ontario conditions with a synthetic Ottawa reservoir. Its storage, inflows and travel time describe the study scenario.

Why did the MPCs agree?

In 2017, anticipating Ottawa changed the predicted Pointe-Claire level for 122 of 176 eligible schedules. Every schedule stayed within its downstream target under both forecasts, so that cost term was zero. The other terms depend on Lake Ontario’s own level and releases. Both rankings therefore gave the same first release on all 48 dates.

In 2019, anticipating Ottawa changed the predicted Pointe-Claire level for 22 of 117 eligible schedules. Every schedule stayed within its downstream target under both forecasts, so that cost term was zero. The other terms depend on Lake Ontario’s own level and releases. Both rankings therefore gave the same first release on all 48 dates.

How we checked the numbers

We reproduced three complete runs for each year at coupled-study revision 9cc8267. We matched the initial state, inflows and issued forecasts across controllers and checked all 48 executed quarter-months in each run. The baseline applies the simulator’s safety filter to the Plan 2014 release proposal.

Spring covers quarter-months 13–24. Flood exposure sums the positive difference between each level and its study threshold over those twelve periods. We checked annual exposure, cost, hydropower value and final Ottawa storage against the saved run summaries. Building counts are peak modeled estimates across the indicator sites in each region.

We paired forecast-based and Stackelberg MPC by year and quarter-month and compared executed releases before rounding. We also replayed each decision and inspected both scores for every eligible schedule. Each scoring rule reproduced its saved first release. Both controllers faced the same Ottawa operator and the same realized inflows when those releases were executed. Candidate scoring

The simulator reports hydropower value and recreational boating impacts in US dollars. We sum its quarter-month indicators for April through June in their original currency. Annual operating cost is a separate weighted score in objective units. Hydropower units · Boating units

↓Project technology

How the project is built

Simulation and control

Python 3.12 runs each controller against the same river simulator. pandas prepares the quarter-month records, NumPy represents release schedules and storage trajectories, and Gymnasium defines the release action space. SciPy checks whether Ottawa can meet the constraints and solves for its lowest-cost feasible release schedule.

Simulation dependencies · Ottawa solver

From verified runs to this page

A Python exporter checks the saved runs and writes a small JSON file. Hugo uses its spring summaries for the flood-exposure comparison, builds static HTML from Story templates and typesets LaTeX with KaTeX. In the browser, JavaScript draws each trajectory from the same file. Chart.js draws the three May 2017 figures and their downloadable PNGs.

CSS variables set the shared palette. Node.js runs the preview and build tools. ESLint checks JavaScript and Stylelint checks CSS. Plotted data · Hugo · Chart.js
